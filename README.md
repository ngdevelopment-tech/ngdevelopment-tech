# Naim Gerges

**Full-Stack Developer · Beirut, Lebanon**

I got into technology from the bottom up. Long before I wrote software that people would use, I was the one opening machines, running cables and sitting with users to figure out why something had stopped working. That habit of understanding how things actually work never left. It pulled me through computer science and algorithms, into building web platforms, then mobile applications, and most recently into intelligent systems that learn and decide.

Today I build complete products. I am just as comfortable shaping the interface a person touches as I am designing the API behind it, modeling the data underneath it, or setting up the automation that keeps it alive. I do not see those as separate jobs for separate people. To me, the whole machine is one job.

## What I do

**Web platforms, end to end.** I take products from an empty folder to production: interfaces, APIs, databases, deployment, and everything in between. I have shipped platforms used by real customers, and I stay to keep them healthy long after launch day.

**Mobile and cross-platform applications.** I build software made for the pocket: quick to open, calm under bad connectivity, and solid enough to survive people who use it every single day.

**AI and intelligent systems.** I work with learning agents, search pipelines and agentic workflows. What interests me is not the demo. It is the engineering that turns a clever model into something dependable.

**Systems and infrastructure.** Networks, security fundamentals, cloud services, hardware. My early years in support and maintenance taught me how every layer actually behaves, so nothing in a stack is a black box to me.

**Experience engineering.** I sweat the last ten percent: motion, typography, interaction, accessibility. Software should feel considered. People notice that, even when they cannot explain why.

## Toolbox

Code is one tool among many. Architecture, interfaces, data, infrastructure and the surrounding tooling decide whether a product holds up, so I reach for whatever the problem needs rather than what habit suggests. The tools change from one project to the next. The engineering discipline does not.
